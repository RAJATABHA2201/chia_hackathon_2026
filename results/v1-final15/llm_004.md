## What I found and why this is the move

I read all three harness-owned integration points before choosing, and two of the three sanctioned levers turn out to be **exhausted or unwired in this tree**:

**T-A is saturated, provably.** `MAC_GATED_TOTAL = 7,864,320` and `macs_issued − macs_useful = 8,388,608 − 524,288 = 7,864,320` — *identical*. There is no ungated zero-operand multiply left to find. `PE.scala` already holds both multiplier inputs *and* the accumulate input, widened to idle cycles. Iteration 2 priced the entire MAC term at 2.12 µJ; against today's 30.1 µJ, a perfect further gate is worth <0.3 µJ.

**T-B cannot remove a cycle here.** `ScratchpadBank` drives `io.sc_zbu_skip` and **nothing in the generator reads that port** — there is no ExecuteController query, contrary to the interface description. `skip` only gates `mem.read(raddr, ren && !sc_skip)` and muxes a zero into the response queue; `io.read.req.ready` and `q.io.enq.valid := RegNext(ren)` are untouched, so issue rate and the preload/compute passes are unchanged by construction. And `CounterFile` exposes exactly one SparseCraft external counter (`MAC_GATED_TOTAL = 8`) — no ZBU event, so suppressed SRAM reads cannot reach the counter-driven energy model. Enabling it buys a measurable zero for ~16K bitmap flops: strictly dominated.

**Time is cycles.** `area_source` ends in `TIMING_UNUSABLE`, and the numbers close on a fixed period: 30.111 µJ / 0.1895 W = 158.9 µs over 79,433 cycles = **2.000 ns exactly**. So Fmax-motivated re-pipelining cannot be scored.

That leaves the cycle count, and the decomposition says where it goes: 0.8953 × 79,433 = 71,116 compute cycles / 2,048 tile-ops = **34.7 cyc/tile-op ≈ 2×DIM**. Half the budget is the preload pass, which costs DIM cycles per tile-op *whatever DIM is* — a term neither `PE.scala` nor the ZBU can touch, and which only amortises by making the tile-op bigger.

The win here is a **hard bound, not an estimate**: a 512×512 A has only 16×16 blocks of 32×32 and X has 2 column blocks, so there are at most 512 tile-ops at DIM=32. Even if coarsening made *every* block nonzero — block-skip reduced to nothing — `512 × 64 = 32,768` versus today's `2,048 × 32 = 65,536`. At least 2× fewer cycles with no assumption about the matrix.

The coupling to T-A is what makes this safe rather than a bare knob turn: a 32×32 block is all-zero far less often, so coarser geometry pushes **more** zeros past the software block-skip and into the array. `macs_issued` rises even as cycles fall — and that surviving population is exactly what the already-saturated zero-gated MAC absorbs.

I deliberately did **not** bundle the scratchpad right-size (256 KB is 3.2× over-provisioned at DIM=32), because it would make the area number unattributable between mesh growth and SRAM shrink. It is step 2.
