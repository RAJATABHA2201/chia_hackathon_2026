**What I found and why this is the move.** The diagnosis points at idle-array/scratchpad-wait, i.e. a *cycle* problem. So I read the harness-owned T-B wiring in `Scratchpad.scala` first. The ZBU's `skip` output feeds exactly two places: the SRAM read-enable (`mem.read(raddr, ren && !sc_skip)`) and a zero-substitution mux on the response. `q.io.enq.valid := RegNext(ren)` and `io.read.req.ready` are **unconditional**, and `ExecuteController.scala` has no ZBU query at all — only a counter block. So T-B as integrated here **cannot remove a single cycle**; it is a pure SRAM-read-energy play that costs a 4096-entry-per-bank bitmap (~16k flops over 4 banks, ~2% of area) plus a 12-bit-decode mux on the SRAM enable path. That is a bad trade against the named cycle levers, which are CONFIG.

The energy axis, by contrast, has measured headroom of exactly `7,864,320 / 8,388,608 = 93.75%`, and the seed T-A in `PE.scala` was leaving most of it on the table: it held only `io.in_a`, isolating the **multiplier**. In the WS datapath `in_b = c1/c2` is the stationary weight (barely toggles) but `in_c = io.in_b` is the partial sum walking down the column, which changes every cycle — so the 32-bit accumulate adder was still switching on all 7.86M gated MACs. That is precisely the "detecting but not isolating" failure mode. I extended the hold to `in_c` behind the same enable, and widened the isolation window to `gated || !in_valid` so the MAC is also quiet across the 61% of cycles the mesh spends idle. Bit-exactness is unchanged: on a gated cycle the existing output mux forwards `b` verbatim, which is identically `b + 0·w`, so the held values never reach an architectural register. I deliberately left the OS branch stock — it is dead at `dataflow = WS` and rewriting it risks an accType/outputType clash at elaboration for zero gain.
