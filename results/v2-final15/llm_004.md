**What the counters actually said.** T-A worked exactly as designed — `MAC_GATED_TOTAL = 7,864,320 / 8,388,608 = 93.75%` — and bought only **6.6%** energy. That is not a broken gate; it is the discovery that the multiplier array was never the dominant term. Gating 93.75% of every multiply in a 16×16 INT8 mesh is worth at most ~2.3 uJ of the 30.1 uJ remaining. Accounting for the rest: DRAM is 327,680 B × ~22 pJ/B ≈ 7.2 uJ, leaving **~20.6 uJ of scratchpad SRAM traffic** — created by iteration 2's `x_resident=1`, which deliberately relocated the dominant energy term from DRAM onto the on-chip SRAM. That relocation was a 66% win and I am not undoing it, but the scratchpad read is now the biggest thing on the chip, and it is precisely what T-B suppresses. T-B has never been measured in this run.

**Granule size is forced, not chosen.** `sc_skip` drives `mem.read(raddr, ren && !sc_skip)`, and that `SyncReadMem` read enable is one bit for the *whole* row. There is no sub-row enable to drive. A granule of 8 or 4 would have to AND its flags back up into the row flag to be consumable — bitwise-identical skips at 2×/4× the bitmap. So `granule_size` stays 16 and the lever I actually moved is **detector pipeline depth**, which the system prompt names as mine.

**The change over the seed ZBU.** The seed put a 128-bit OR-reduce in series with a 4096-entry one-hot write decoder in a single cycle, while the flat bitmap's 4096:1 read mux already sits in series with the SRAM read enable — both halves on the same period, on the axis T-B is most likely to lose (t is cycles × *measured* period). I split it: cycle 0 reduces and latches `(addr, bit)`, cycle 1 commits. The one hazard a late update creates is the dangerous direction — a pending write turning a zero row nonzero while the stale bit is still SET would skip real data — so `hazard = pend_valid && pend_addr === read_addr` kills the skip in the shadow cycle, degrading to a dense read. The converse case only costs a missed skip. The comparator is in *parallel* with the bitmap mux, adding one AND rather than a second mux tree.

I also took `time: flat` as given rather than hunting a speedup: the `preload`/`compute` suppression that would buy 2×DIM cycles lives in `ExecuteController.scala`, which is not writable.
